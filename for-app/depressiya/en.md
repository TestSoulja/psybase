## What this changes for Opora

1. **A separate condition ≠ "a bad day".** The threshold from the definition —
   at least 2 weeks, most of the day, nearly every day — is the only thing that
   separates an episode from an evening. The diary needs the label "lasts ≥2 weeks
   in a row", not "depression today". Otherwise the app itself devalues the word.
2. **A "what to say to those close to you" screen — with a banned list and the
   reason.** Not allowed: "pull yourself together", "that's laziness", "it's all in
   your head", "others have it worse", "just go for a walk". The honest replacement
   is the NHS wording: it is not weakness and not something you "snap out of". The
   reason can be stated: such phrases are the criticism in question; an association
   with worse symptoms exists, causation is not proven (say so).
3. **A prognosis without promises.** "It often passes by itself within months, and
   in some people it comes back" — that is 50% with no recurrence, 35% with
   recurrence, 15% with no clear year. "Everything will be fine" is an invention of
   the same kind as an invented address.
4. **Do not advise treatment and do not cancel it.** The app may say
   "psychological treatments are first-line, and for mild depression medicines are
   not required", with a WHO reference, but the choice belongs to the doctor.

## What changes in Opora

- **Diary:** the label "lasts ≥2 weeks in a row" instead of "depression today";
  there is no separate "bad day" condition.
- **Screen for those close:** banned phrases with the reason and an honest
  replacement; sources — [`nhs-depression-overview`](../sources/nhs-depression-overview.md, `review`)
  and [`li-2025-criticism`](../sources/li-2025-criticism.md, `cohort`).
- **Prognosis:** without "everything will be fine"; the three numbers together
  [`eaton-2008-baltimore`](../sources/eaton-2008-baltimore.md, `cohort`).
- **Treatment:** only a description of what services recommend
  [`who-2025-depression`](../sources/who-2025-depression.md, `review`); no
  prescriptions, doses or advice to stop anything.

## Not found

- No study tests the exact phrase "pull yourself together": criticism was measured
  as general perceived criticism, not as specific words.
- No validated Russian-language data on how such phrases land in Russia — English
  numbers cannot be transferred to Russian wording.
