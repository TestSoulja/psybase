## What it changes for "Opora"

1. **Labels are about timing, not action.** Honest: "the effect accumulates; the
   first changes may appear by the end of week 1, and it is too early to judge
   before 4–6 weeks". Dishonest: "it helps", "it does not help", "you should feel
   better".
2. **Start date and day of intake are mandatory** — otherwise the app cannot
   show "day 12, still early". A separate line about stopping: "do not stop
   abruptly; the tapering schedule is prescribed by a doctor".
3. **A missed-dose mark is needed, a judgement is not.** A missed dose is
   recorded as a fact; the app does not infer "the treatment is failing" and does
   not turn adherence into a reproach.
4. **What cannot be derived from the records:** "it does not suit me", "the dose
   is too low", "it is time to stop". That is the doctor's conclusion; the diary
   shows only the person's own records and their dates.

## What changes in "Opora"

- **A medication card:** name, start date, schedule — without an efficacy rating
  [`cipriani-2018-21-antidepressants`](../sources/cipriani-2018-21-antidepressants.md, `meta`).
- **A missed-dose reminder:** a fact, not a reproach; no streaks or adherence
  percentages.
- **Text about stopping:** "do not stop abruptly" and "the tapering schedule is
  prescribed by a doctor", without specific timelines — the right schedule is
  individual
  [`horowitz-taylor-2019-tapering`](../sources/horowitz-taylor-2019-tapering.md, `review`).
- **Prognosis:** no "it will cure you" and no "you will have to take it for
  life"; what happens after a year is not in the verified sources
  [`stone-2022-fda-ipd`](../sources/stone-2022-fda-ipd.md, `meta`).
