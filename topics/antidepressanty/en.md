## What it is

Antidepressants are a class of medicines prescribed for depression to reduce the
symptoms of an episode. They are not "mood pills" and not stimulants: the effect
accumulates over weeks, and the decision to start, to change the dose or to stop
is made by a doctor
[`cipriani-2018-21-antidepressants`](../sources/cipriani-2018-21-antidepressants.md, `meta`).

What follows is what meta-analyses and reviews show — and what they do not.

## What is known

**All 21 drugs beat placebo, but the differences between them are small.** 522
RCTs, 116 477 people: response OR from 2.13 (amitriptyline, 1.89–2.41) to 1.37
(reboxetine, 1.16–1.63); differences between drugs in efficacy — 1.15–1.55. High
risk of bias in 46 (9%) trials, moderate in 380 (73%), low in 96 (18%); the
authors' certainty was moderate to very low
[`cipriani-2018-21-antidepressants`](../sources/cipriani-2018-21-antidepressants.md, `meta`).

**The mean difference from placebo is 1.75 HAMD points.** 232 RCTs, 73 388
people, FDA data 1979–2016: 1.75 (1.63–1.86). A large response in 24.5% versus
9.6%, a minimal one in 12.2% versus 21.5%; "about 15% have a substantial effect
beyond placebo". There is no trend over the years: the placebo response did not
grow [`stone-2022-fda-ipd`](../sources/stone-2022-fda-ipd.md, `meta`).

**The difference grows with severity.** At HDRS below 23 the difference is less
than d 0.20 — a "small effect"; the NICE threshold of clinical significance is
crossed at HDRS 25
[`fournier-2010-severity`](../sources/fournier-2010-severity.md, `meta`). The FDA
data show the same: the difference grows but remains small even in very severe
depression, and the reason is a fall in the placebo response, not a rise in the
drug response [`kirsch-2008-severity`](../sources/kirsch-2008-severity.md, `meta`).

**Improvement is visible by the end of the first week.** 28 RCTs, n = 5872: a
50% fall in HAMD within a week — RR 1.64 (1.2–2.25); after that improvement
continues at a decreasing rate for at least 6 weeks
[`taylor-2006-early-response`](../sources/taylor-2006-early-response.md, `meta`).
Caveat: these are SSRIs, not the whole class.

**Fewer dropouts than placebo — only for two drugs.** Fewer discontinuations than
on placebo with agomelatine (OR 0.84; 0.72–0.97) and fluoxetine (0.88;
0.80–0.96); more with clomipramine (1.30; 1.01–1.68). In head-to-head
comparisons the highest dropout was with seven drugs — OR 1.30–2.32
[`cipriani-2018-21-antidepressants`](../sources/cipriani-2018-21-antidepressants.md, `meta`).

**Stopping is not "a couple of weeks".** 24 studies: incidence 27–86%, weighted
average 56%; 46% of those who experienced it rated the severity as extreme. The
mean duration in four studies was 5 days, 10 days, 43 days and 79 weeks; 7 of 10
studies of duration contradict the guidelines' claim that withdrawal is
self-limiting within 1–2 weeks
[`davies-read-2019-withdrawal`](../sources/davies-read-2019-withdrawal.md, `review`).

**Short tapering schedules do not solve the problem.** Guidelines suggest
tapering over 2–4 weeks down to the therapeutic minimum; such schedules are
minimally better than abrupt withdrawal and are often poorly tolerated. More
successful are tapers over months and down to doses below the therapeutic
minimum, hyperbolically: PET data show that serotonin transporter occupancy falls
linearly with such tapering
[`horowitz-taylor-2019-tapering`](../sources/horowitz-taylor-2019-tapering.md, `review`).

**Switching medication.** The share of people who feel worse specifically after
a switch is not reported in the studies found. What is measured is
discontinuation symptoms — after stopping, not after switching. 50 studies,
17 828 people; in the DESS meta-analysis (11 studies, 3915 people) symptoms in
the first week after stopping were greater than on placebo — SMD 0.31 (0.23–0.39),
equivalent to "one more symptom"; the most frequent were dizziness (OR 5.52;
3.81–8.01), nausea (3.16), vertigo (6.40), nervousness (3.15); dizziness was the
most prevalent symptom (risk difference 6.24%). In that study discontinuation was
not associated with depression symptoms: the authors attribute later worsening
to relapse, not to discontinuation
[`kalfas-2025-discontinuation`](../sources/kalfas-2025-discontinuation.md, `meta`).
Another estimate — 79 studies, 21 002 people: at least one discontinuation
symptom in 31% versus 17% on placebo (difference 8%), severe in 2.8% versus
0.6%; allowing for non-specific effects, about 15%, one in six to seven
[`henssler-2024-incidence`](../sources/henssler-2024-incidence.md, `meta`). This
is not the same as the 56% above: there 24 studies and a range of 27–86%, here
79 studies and a pooled share; the numbers answer different questions and stand
side by side rather than replace each other.

The DESS scale used to measure these symptoms is reliable, but in 63 inpatients
it poorly separated discontinuation from depression and side effects: scores did
not differ between those who stopped and those who took no antidepressant, fell
over time, and were highest before the dose reduction
[`mass-2026-dess`](../sources/mass-2026-dess.md, `cohort`).

How often switching happens at all: 15.0% of children and adolescents aged 5–18
switched to another antidepressant within 12 months (44 381 people, Australia);
the reasons for switching are not recorded in the data
[`kassie-2026-switching`](../sources/kassie-2026-switching.md, `cohort`). Direct
switching from an SSRI was studied in one 8-week double-blind RCT — in people
with sexual dysfunction; the overall share who worsened is not reported
[`jacobsen-2020-direct-switch`](../sources/jacobsen-2020-direct-switch.md, `rct`).
Rapid switching between MAOIs is described in three cases, all without events
[`rached-2025-maoi-switch`](../sources/rached-2025-maoi-switch.md, `case`): an
illustration, not a rate.

**A dose is not the same as a drug.** The share of people who feel worse
specifically after a dose increase is not reported in the studies found. The
closest measure is activation (jitteriness) at treatment start: 27.7% over
6 weeks in 209 people, of whom 6.7% in the first 2 weeks; a high dose was
associated with activation (OR 2.68; 1.37–5.25)
[`sinha-2017-jitteriness`](../sources/sinha-2017-jitteriness.md, `cohort`). A
review of 107 articles gives a range of 4–65% and states outright that
definitions differ
[`sinclair-2009-jitteriness-review`](../sources/sinclair-2009-jitteriness-review.md, `review`).
Prospectively, in 301 people — 7.0% in the first month
[`harada-2014-jitteriness`](../sources/harada-2014-jitteriness.md, `cohort`). A
meta-analysis of dose increase (9 RCTs, 1273 people) found no effect — SMD 0.053
(−0.143–0.248), but it is about efficacy, not about the share who worsen
[`rink-2018-dose-increase`](../sources/rink-2018-dose-increase.md, `meta`).
Switching into mania is a different outcome, covered below.

**In BPD there are no separate data.** The discontinuation reviews were
recruited in depression and anxiety disorders, the switching reviews in
depression; their shares cannot be transferred to BPD.

**Bipolar disorder: the fear of a switch to mania is not confirmed by numbers.**
13 RCTs, 1362 patients: no antidepressant raised the risk significantly against
placebo. The maximum was venlafaxine, RR 4.53 (0.47–43.25): not significant, but
the only consistent signal. Heterogeneity was low, confidence was low
[`oliva-2025-bipolar-switch`](../sources/oliva-2025-bipolar-switch.md, `meta`).

**In BPD there is no indication, yet most patients are prescribed one.** "No
approved pharmacological treatment for BPD exists"; antidepressants are the most
frequent class at baseline, 80.4%; polypharmacy (by the authors' methods, more
than one medication at the same time; the abstract says "≥ 1") in 83.1%. This
is a retrospective cohort: 12-month data were available for 1461 people (16.1%
of those who started)
[`white-2026-bpd-antidepressants`](../sources/white-2026-bpd-antidepressants.md, `cohort`).

## What is not proven

- **Long term.** The largest studies cover the acute phase, about 8 weeks. What
  happens to the effect after a year is not in these sources.
- **"Placebo works too" is not fully refuted.** Both are true: the drugs beat
  placebo, and in mild and moderate depression the difference is below the
  threshold of clinical significance. The two statements stand side by side;
  neither replaces the other.
- **Individual response is unpredictable.** Variability is acknowledged; there
  is no predictor for a particular person.
- **The exact share who stop because of side effects.** The meta-analysis
  reports discontinuation for any cause (OR) only.
- **NNT.** We do not publish an NNT from these data: it depends on baseline risk
  and follow-up time, not only on the difference in proportions. It is not in
  the papers, so it is not here either.

## Not found

- **NICE NG222** — 403 on every attempt (page and PDF). NICE wording is not
  quoted, so as not to retell it from memory.
- **EMA and FDA primary documents on the incidence of withdrawal** — not
  opened.
- **The share who feel worse specifically after switching medication** — not
  found: a search on 25.11.2026 in Europe PMC
  (`TITLE:antidepressant AND (TITLE:switching OR TITLE:switch)`,
  `ABSTRACT:"switch" AND ABSTRACT:"adverse events" AND ABSTRACT:antidepressant`,
  `TITLE:"discontinuation emergent"`) returned no study with that share.
- **RCTs of switching strategies with adverse events as an outcome** — not
  found: comparisons report depression symptoms, switching frequency or
  individual cases; the outcome "share who worsened" is absent.
- **BPD data separate from the general samples** — not found: the
  discontinuation reviews were recruited in depression and anxiety disorders,
  the switching reviews in depression.
- **The share who feel worse specifically after a dose increase** — not found:
  a search on 25.11.2026 in Europe PMC
  (`(TITLE:"dose increase" OR TITLE:"dose escalation") AND (TITLE:antidepressant OR TITLE:SSRI OR TITLE:serotonin)`,
  `TITLE:"activation syndrome" AND (TITLE:SSRI OR TITLE:antidepressant OR TITLE:serotonergic)`,
  `(ABSTRACT:jitteriness OR ABSTRACT:"activation syndrome") AND ABSTRACT:antidepressant`)
  returned activation rates at treatment start and an association with high
  dose, but not that share.
- **A Cochrane review of antidepressants in personality disorders** — not
  found.
- **RCTs of antidepressants in BPD** — not opened; the topic covers prescribing
  practice only.
- **Russian-language data** — none found; all numbers above come from the USA,
  the UK or international meta-analyses.
